Why DreamPlayer

The difference is what reaches your screen

Two things decide whether a player is actually good: whether the picture stays HDR all the way to the panel, and whether you can reach your files without installing anything else.

HDR that stays HDR

Video is rendered into a real hardware surface, so Dolby Vision P7/P8/P9, HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG reach the display as PQ/BT.2020 instead of being tone-mapped to SDR in software. On panels that support it, you get the highlights the file actually has.

Media3 · AetherEngine · SurfaceView

Your NAS, inside the app

SMB is built in on every platform, including iPhone and iPad. Browse shares, open a file, seek instantly, and keep playing when the network wobbles — no companion SMB utility, no separate login.

SMB 3.1.1 · libsmb2 · jcifs-ng

Lossless, not downmixed

DTS-HD Master Audio, TrueHD, E-AC3, AC3 and 24-bit multichannel FLAC are decoded properly — and passed through bit-for-bit over HDMI when the receiver can take it. Atmos and DTS:X included.

No streaming-service DRM

DreamPlayer plays files you have the right to play — yours, your NAS, your Jellyfin. It does not and will not decrypt Netflix or other Widevine/FairPlay streams. That is a deliberate line, not a missing feature.

FAQ

Straight answers

Does it really do true Dolby Vision, or tone-map it?

On Android, playback runs through Media3 into a hardware surface, so Dolby Vision and HDR10 signals are handed to the display rather than converted to SDR in software. On iOS, playback uses the native AVPlayer path for the same reason. On a panel without Dolby Vision, P7/P8 profiles play as HDR10 HEVC — which is what those base layers are — instead of failing.

Do I need a separate SMB app on iPhone or iPad?

No. DreamPlayer speaks SMB itself on every platform, so your NAS is browsable and playable without installing anything else.

Can it play Netflix, Prime Video or other streaming services?

No, and that is intentional. Those streams are DRM-protected and DreamPlayer does not implement Widevine or FairPlay. It plays files you own or have rights to.

Which subtitles are supported?

Embedded tracks in the file, plus every subtitle sitting next to it — SRT, ASS/SSA, WebVTT, TTML, SAMI, MicroDVD, MPL2, SubViewer, PGS and DVB bitmap ones. Sidecars are matched to the video automatically, and OpenSubtitles search is built in for when they are not.

Does it work on a TV?

Yes — Android TV is supported, including Fire TV Stick 4K, with full Dolby Vision and audio passthrough to the panel and soundbar. The app is the same build as the phone.

Is there any account or tracking?

No. There is no sign-in, no analytics and no advertising SDK. Your library, history and metadata stay on your device.

Why is it free on Android but paid on iOS?

Android builds are published on GitHub Releases and cost nothing. On iOS, Apple requires in-app purchase for anything unlocked inside the app, so the optional features are sold there. The Android build is never gated.

Is it open source?

Yes, GPLv3. The source is on GitHub, and issues and pull requests are welcome — including the ones that turn out to be hardware quirks nobody else has hit yet.
